Depreciation report vs reserve fund study: is there a difference?

By Ashgrove Strata Advisors · Updated November 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • A depreciation report and a reserve fund study are the same kind of document under different provincial names.
  • In BC the legal term is depreciation report. Alberta and Ontario use reserve fund study.
  • An insurance appraisal answers a different question and does not replace a depreciation report.

Councils and managers who have worked in other provinces often ask whether BC's depreciation report is something different from a reserve fund study. It is not.

Same document, different name

Both describe a long-term plan for the shared parts of a building. A qualified professional inspects the property, lists the components the corporation must repair and replace, estimates when each will need work and what it will cost, and models how the reserve fund can pay for it.

British Columbia's Strata Property Act calls this a depreciation report, and the money is held in the contingency reserve fund. Alberta and Ontario call the document a reserve fund study. The details set by each province differ, such as how often it must be renewed and who may prepare it, but the purpose is the same.

How it differs from an insurance appraisal

Depreciation reportInsurance appraisal
Question it answersWhat will repairs and replacements cost over 30 years, and how do we fund them?What would it cost to rebuild the buildings today after a loss?
Used forBudgets, contingency reserve fund contributions and maintenance planningSetting the insured value on the strata's policy
How oftenAt least every five years in BCOften updated yearly at the insurer's request

Many stratas order both at once, because one site visit can serve both assignments.

How it differs from a building condition assessment

A building condition assessment or an engineering investigation looks closely at one system or one problem, such as a building envelope or a parkade membrane, sometimes with testing. A depreciation report is based on a visual inspection of all common property and focuses on timing and funding. When a depreciation report flags a concern, a targeted engineering review is often the next step.

Which one do you need?

If your strata has five or more lots, the depreciation report is the one the Act requires. The others are tools you add when insurance or a specific building problem calls for them.

This guide is general information for BC strata corporations, not legal advice. For how the Strata Property Act applies to your strata, speak with your strata manager or a lawyer. Official rules: Province of BC, strata depreciation report requirements.
