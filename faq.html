Questions

Depreciation reports in BC: common questions

Based on the Strata Property Act and Regulation as of November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

How often does a BC strata need a depreciation report?

Every five years, for strata corporations with five or more lots, including bare land stratas. Stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt.

Can our strata waive the depreciation report?

No. Since July 1, 2024, strata corporations can no longer defer the report with a ¾ vote at the annual general meeting.

Is our strata behind on its report?

If your strata has no report, or its last report is dated before December 31, 2020, the deadline was July 1, 2026 in Metro Vancouver, the Fraser Valley and the Capital Regional District, and is July 1, 2027 in the rest of BC.

Is a depreciation report the same as a reserve fund study?

In substance, yes. BC's Strata Property Act calls it a depreciation report; Ontario, Alberta and most other provinces call the same kind of study a reserve fund study, and in the United States it is a reserve study. How often it is required, who may prepare it and what it must contain differ by province. Our reports follow BC's requirements.

Who can prepare a depreciation report in BC?

Under section 94 of the Strata Property Act, the report must come from a "qualified person", defined in section 6.2 of the Strata Property Regulation. Since July 1, 2025, that means one of six professional groups: professional engineers, architects or architectural technologists, applied science technologists, Accredited Appraisers (AACI) of the Appraisal Institute of Canada, Certified Reserve Planners, and Professional Quantity Surveyors.

How much does a depreciation report cost in BC?

Our fixed fees start at $2,000 for a townhouse, $3,000 for a low-rise and $5,000 for a high-rise, before GST. Commercial, industrial and sectioned stratas are quoted individually. Complete records, such as financial statements with reserve fund details and invoices for major work, lower the fee. See pricing

What does a depreciation report include?

An on-site visual inspection, an inventory of the common property and assets the strata maintains with their expected life, a 30-year forecast of repair and replacement costs, at least three 30-year funding models, the current contingency reserve fund balance, and an executive summary.

How long does a depreciation report take?

We usually inspect within three weeks of receiving your deposit, and deliver the draft report about four weeks after the inspection and receipt of your records.

Do owners have to follow the funding models?

No. The funding models are options for council and owners to consider. Contribution changes and special levies are decided by owners under the Strata Property Act.
